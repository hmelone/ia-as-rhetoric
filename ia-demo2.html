Rhetorical element: Navigation bar
Ethos — Consistent navigation Every page carries the same nav bar in the same order. Consistency is a form of ethos: it signals the system is coherent and trustworthy. Salvo argues IA constructs "the conditions under which users engage." A nav that never changes proves the system will behave the same way every time.

Rhetorical element: Breadcrumb trail
Pathos — Location cues reduce anxiety "Home › IA & Rhetoric" tells users exactly where they are. Matloobtalab and Ferati's participants described the absence of this as: "You do not get any clues in the interface." Structural clarity is an affective intervention. Knowing where you are is calming; not knowing is destabilizing.

Rhetorical element: Section label
Logos — Establishing sequence "Part One" signals position in a larger argument before the reader has seen any content. This is logos in structural form: hierarchy implies order, and order implies logic. The label constructs a sense of reasoned progression before the argument has even begun.

Rhetorical element: Page heading (H1)
Logos + Ethos — Specific, committed labeling Rosenfeld et al. observe that "no label totally captures the meaning of a document," yet labels must be chosen. This heading is specific ("as Rhetorical Practice"), not generic ("Overview"). Specificity signals a deliberate interpretive commitment and invites the reader to evaluate the argument rather than guess at it.

Rhetorical element: Lede paragraph
Logos + Pathos — Redish’s layering The lede gives readers enough to orient before committing to the full section, which is consistent with Redish’s principle of layering. The border-left accent visually marks it as entry-point content, not body text. The structural cue manages reader attention: it says "start here, go deeper." The design is doing rhetorical work.

Rhetorical element: Pull quote
Ethos + Logos — Attributed authority, floated for emphasis The pull quote does two things: it attributes a claim to a named authority (ethos through citation), and its float-right position means the reader encounters it mid-prose, creating a visual argument where the text and quote reinforce each other simultaneously. Position is part of the argument.

Rhetorical element: Body prose with embedded link
Logos — Embedded links vs. “click here” The link text reads "information architecture," not "click here." This signals that the term has a definition worth consulting without interrupting the argumentative flow. Navigation is embedded in the prose logic, not appended to it.

Rhetorical element: Sidebar key terms
Logos — Taxonomy before technology Dalkir’s principle "taxonomy before technology" holds that conceptual structure precedes tools. The sidebar key terms panel instantiates this: it provides interpretive vocabulary before (or while) the reader encounters the argument. Terms link to the glossary. This is IA as proactive knowledge support, not reactive labeling.

What the Research Predicts — and What You Experienced

Every failure in the bad-IA site corresponds to a finding in the research. Every success in the good site enacts a rhetorical principle from the scholarship.

✓ Good IA — What Works and Why

🧭
Breadcrumbs on every page
Prevents the disorientation Matloobtalab & Ferati found: "You do not get any clues in the interface."
📐
Consistent heading hierarchy
Encodes logos: H1 → H2 → H3 creates an interpretive sequence users can trust without thinking about it.
🔗
Embedded prose links
"Information architecture" rather than "click here" preserves argumentative flow while signaling definitional depth.
📚
Attributed pull quotes
Each pull quote names its source — constructing ethos through citation, not decoration.
🏛
Glossary linked from prose
Follows Dalkir’s "taxonomy before technology" — the interpretive vocabulary is provided before the reader needs it.

✗ Bad IA — What Fails and Why

🔄
No breadcrumbs anywhere
The reader has no location cue on any page. There is no way to know where you are in the site without using the browser’s back button.
🔃
Flat heading hierarchy
H1, H2, and H3 are all 17px — identical in size. The reader cannot distinguish the page title from a section from a subsection. Structure communicates nothing.
❓
Vertical nav with meaningless labels
Navigation links stack vertically and are labeled Section 1.1, Section 0, Section 2 — giving no indication of content or sequence.
📊
Oversized pull quote with no citation
The pull quote runs nearly full width and appears before the text it quotes. Two of three have no attribution — the reader cannot evaluate the claim.
🚪
References buried with no heading
Citations appear at the bottom with no section break or hanging indents, blending into body text and nearly invisible to the reader.
The thesis, enacted: You have just experienced what this paper argues. The good site constructed conditions under which knowledge could be encountered and trusted. The bad site dismantled those conditions. The content was never the variable. The structure was.
