Reference & Further Reading

References & Resources

The following sources are cited throughout this site. Where electronic versions are available, titles link directly to publisher pages or open-access records.

Dalkir, Kimiz. Knowledge Management in Theory and Practice. 2nd ed., MIT Press, 2011. Provides the framework for understanding taxonomies as rhetorical constructs and supplies Koenig’s “taxonomy before technology” formulation that anchors the Codification section’s argument.

Davenport, Thomas H., and Laurence Prusak. Working Knowledge: How Organizations Manage What They Know. Harvard Business School Press, 1998. Locates knowledge in the knower rather than the repository, establishing the foundational distinction between tacit and explicit knowledge and the inherent loss involved in codification.

Matloobtalab, Mehrnaz, and Mexhid Ferati. “User Perspectives and Usability Insights in a Self-Service Portal.” International Journal of Web Portals, vol. 15, no. 1, 2025. https://doi.org/10.4018/IJWP.372057. Empirical study of a failing university portal that supplies the case material for the Usability & Trust section, including the participant testimony of structural failure becoming a collapse of confidence.

Rosenfeld, Louis, Peter Morville, and Jorge Arango. Information Architecture for the Web and Beyond. 4th ed., O’Reilly Media, 2015. Defines information architecture and establishes the instability of information itself, which the IA & Rhetoric section uses to argue that meaning is not transferred but constructed.

Ruzza, Mirko, et al. “Designing the Information Architecture of a Complex Website: A Strategy Based on News Content and Faceted Classification.” International Journal of Information Management, vol. 37, 2017, pp. 166–176. Healthcare-website case study that supplies the Structural Models section with its primary comparison of hierarchical and faceted classification and the hybrid approach that pairs them.

Salvo, Michael J. “Rhetorical Action in Professional Space: Information Architecture as Critical Practice.” Journal of Business and Technical Communication, vol. 18, no. 1, 2004, pp. 39–66. JBTC. The core intervention of the site. Salvo reframes IA as “a site of intervention” rather than a neutral technical practice, providing the rhetorical theory that organizes the entire argument.

Further Reading

The following external resources are recommended for further reading on information architecture, usability, and knowledge management:

  • Nielsen Norman Group: “IA vs. Navigation”. Research-based guidance on the distinction between information architecture and navigation design.
  • Knowledge Management Institute: “Information Architecture and KM”. A practitioner-oriented overview of the relationship between information architecture and knowledge management, extending the arguments developed in the Knowledge Codification section of this site.
  • ASIS&T (Association for Information Science and Technology). The premier academic society for information science research. The scholarship on information architecture, classification, and knowledge organization that underpins this site’s argument lives largely within this community.
