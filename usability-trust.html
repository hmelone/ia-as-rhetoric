Part Four

When IA Fails: Usability, Trust, and the Collapse of Ethos

The rhetorical character of information architecture becomes most visible when that architecture fails. When structural cues are unclear or inconsistent, users do not experience mere technical inconvenience. They lose the ability to interpret the system at all, and with it, their confidence in what the system tells them.

A University Portal: Structural Failure in Practice

Matloobtalab and Ferati's study of a university self-service portal illustrates this dynamic concretely. Using cognitive walkthrough, usability testing, and semi-structured interviews, the researchers assessed how users navigated and interpreted the portal's structure. Across multiple participants, a consistent pattern emerged: users described the interface as lacking navigational cues and struggling to communicate its organizational logic.

One participant observed that the system was "not really coherent … not very straightforward … You do not get any clues in the interface." Another expressed ongoing uncertainty about whether actions had been correctly completed: "I don't know, like now I've done it 5 minutes ago. I still do not know where to put it [the request]. I have not learned."

Most tellingly, one participant articulated not just confusion but distrust of the system's basic reliability: "My only thought when using those kinds of websites is that I am not sure if my main goal is to send the message [request] to the correct place."

"My only thought when using those kinds of websites is that I am not sure if my main goal is to send the message [request] to the correct place." — Study participant, Matloobtalab & Ferati, 2025

A Collapse of Ethos

This last comment is particularly significant for a rhetorical analysis of IA. The user is not only uncertain about where to navigate, but also whether the system has processed an action correctly at all. This is a collapse of ethos. The structural cues that would normally signal coherence and trustworthiness have failed, and the user has no basis for confidence that the system is working as intended.

The result is not inefficiency alone but a fundamental breakdown in the communicative relationship between system and user. The system is no longer a credible communicator. It cannot be trusted to confirm what the user has done, where they are, or whether their actions have had the intended effect.

Quantitative Confirmation

Matloobtalab and Ferati's quantitative data confirms this pattern. They found a strong negative correlation between system usability scores and the number of clicks required to complete tasks (r = −0.737, p < 0.05). Users who needed more clicks, indicating navigational confusion, also reported substantially lower satisfaction with the system overall.

The structural failures that produced excess clicks were not experienced as impartial difficulty. They were experienced as a degraded relationship with the system itself. Navigation is not merely functional. It is communicative. When it breaks down, users feel that the system has failed to engage with them as competent agents.

Abandonment as Rational Response

The broader implication is striking. When a knowledge system's structural logic cannot be interpreted, users abandon it for alternatives they trust more. Matloobtalab and Ferati found that participants overwhelmingly preferred to bypass the portal entirely and approach IT staff directly, not because the portal lacked the right content, but because its structure failed to communicate that content reliably.

"If IA constructs the conditions under which communication occurs, a failed IA dismantles those conditions — and users rationally seek other means of communication." — After Salvo, 2004; Matloobtalab & Ferati, 2025

This pattern confirms Salvo's argument in reverse. If IA constructs the conditions under which communication occurs, a failed IA dismantles those conditions, and users rationally seek other means of communication. The self-service system fails precisely because its structural logic cannot sustain the communicative relationship it was designed to replace.

Conclusions: Structure as Responsibility

The convergence of evidence across these sources points toward a conclusion with practical and theoretical implications for technical communication and information design. Information architecture is not a technical background condition for knowledge delivery. It is a rhetorical system that constructs the interpretive conditions under which users encounter, evaluate, and act on information.

As organizations increasingly rely on self-service documentation and web-based knowledge bases, and as AI-driven search and content generation become integrated into these systems, the rhetorical stakes of information architecture will only grow. The structures that organize knowledge systems will determine not only what users can find, but what they are able to understand and believe.

Salvo's insistence that IA should be understood as a "site of intervention" rather than an objective organizational practice is, in this light, more than a theoretical reframe. It is a call for designers to take responsibility for the interpretive work their structures perform. Recognizing this is the first step toward designing knowledge systems that serve users not just efficiently, but meaningfully.
