Introduction

Structure Is Not Neutral

Web-based knowledge systems are typically evaluated on usability, content accuracy, and navigational efficiency. Such evaluations treat information architecture as a neutral technical layer. But the structural decisions that constitute an information architecture are persuasive acts, not merely logistical ones.

Help centers, documentation portals, and self-service platforms have become primary interfaces through which users encounter institutional knowledge. How that knowledge is organized, labeled, and hierarchically arranged determines what users see, how they understand relationships between concepts, and whether they trust what they find.

This site argues that information architecture functions as a form of rhetorical practice. Drawing on scholarship in technical communication, knowledge management, and information science, it examines how classification, taxonomy, hierarchy, and navigation operate rhetorically — constructing meaning, directing attention, and establishing credibility.

Key Scholars

Rosenfeld, Morville & Arango Define information architecture and establish that information is inherently unstable, requiring a receiver to make sense of it.
Michael J. Salvo Argues IA is a “site of intervention,” an active rhetorical practice rather than a passive technical function.
Davenport & Prusak Locate knowledge in the person, not the repository, establishing why codification decisions are consequential rhetorical acts.
